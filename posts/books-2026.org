I've been reading a bunch over the past year, mostly fantasy series.

 *{{{foldstart(The Heritage of Shannara)}}}*
By Terry Brooks:

- The Scions of Shannara (1990)
- The Druid of Shannara (1991)
- The Elf Queen of Shannara (1992)
- The Talismans of Shannara (1993)
{{{foldend}}}

I revisited The Sword of Shannara for the first time since high school as a comfort read, and was inspired to check out this quartet further down the line. It was easy reading and fun. Incredible to visit MORROWIND of all places. In the aftermath of the 2nd book, it occurred to me that the 2nd of any trilogy is the best. You don't have the world rampup needed of the first, you get a tantalizing peek at the potential for the future, and (if you're lucky) you get an epic journey on the way.

 *{{{foldstart(The Stormlight Archive 1-3)}}}*
By Brandon Sanderson:

- The Way of Kings (2010)
- Words of Radiance (2014)
- Oathbringer (2017)
{{{foldend}}}

Pretty epic. As one commenter summed up, "big crabs and depression". There are 10 planned total books and only 5 are written right now - I don't feel any rush to complete books 4 and 5. The Sanderlanche is awesome, but MAN does he spend a lot of time exploring the characters past. Reading these books feels comfy, sometimes boring, but the payoff is always there at the end.

 *{{{foldstart(The Hierarchy 1-2)}}}*
By James Islington:

- The Will of the Many (2023)
- The Strength of the few (2025)
{{{foldend}}}

I started this series annoyed at how much I was enjoying it, it was as if someone smashed Name of the Wind into Red Rising (with elements of The Sum of All Men) . It definitely is it's own thing though, and has some JUICY payoffs, I felt like I was watching an action movie. Zipped through all the currently written books fast enough I got pissed, but James has a draft of the next book written at the time of this writing. Very curious to see where the series goes from here.

 *{{{foldstart(Malazan 1-2.5)}}}*
 By Steven Erikson:

- Gardens of the Moon (1999)
- Deadhouse Gates (2000)
- /currently reading/: Memories of Ice (2001)
{{{foldend}}}

There are ten books to the main series here, and at the end of the second one I feel a deep hunger to finish them all. I'm still adjusting to the setting, and while baffled by many things, reading these books has made me laugh/cry/wtf? plenty of times already. Perhaps for 2027, the Malazan world will be my shaved knuckle in the hole.
